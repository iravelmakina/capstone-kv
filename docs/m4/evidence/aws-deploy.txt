===== What the lab found, and where it went =====
- D2 against ADR-0026: one node held both leaders, and the closed-loop client turned
  14.7 s of 503 into ≈ 0.93% of an hour's requests. -> ADR-0030 (accepted): availability
  99% per hour. D1' shows the other side of it: a stopped follower node costs nothing.
- The burn-rate alerts fired on a handful of errors at almost no traffic (S5). -> ADR-0030:
  budget alerts only at >= 1 request/s; rules_test.yml covers it.
- Prometheus and Loki lost their data on a lab restart (R1): Prometheus got a new
  anonymous volume per container, Loki kept its data inside the container. -> monitor-boot.sh
  puts both on the instance's disk. Checked locally, then in AWS (session 3, [M]).
- smoke-replica couldn't pass under write load (D1'). -> its target is now each leader's
  last record 6 s after the node is back. Checked locally under ~390 writes/s (old: FAIL
  after 123 s, new: ok in 6 s), then in AWS under ~900 requests/s (session 3, [D'']).
- Two leaders on one node: left for M5 (ADR-0030's runner-up).
- Write p99 between 30 and 60 ms was interpolated (no bucket in between, ADR-0025). ->
  ADR-0031: buckets at 40 and 50 ms (and 6, 10, 20); in AWS write p99 41.0 ms against the
  client's 42.9 ms (session 3, [P]).
- Grafana's Alertmanager health check (500): Grafana's plugin has no server-side check.
  -> `make grafana-check` checks it through the proxy; PASS in AWS (session 3, [V]).
- Loki grows ≈ 0.8 GB an hour under benchmark load on an 8 GB monitor disk: a risk for
  M5's longer load tests (session 3, [Disk]).
